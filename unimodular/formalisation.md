# The formalisation

The formalisation uses ordinary lattice vectors, convex cones, finite fans, polytopes, Mathlib matroids, and schemes. Supporting constructions are connected to these objects by proved equalities, equivalences, and geometric identifications.

## Complete fans and the positive degree-one equations

Fix a finite family of nonzero integral vectors positively spanning a finite-dimensional real lattice space. An *allowed block* is a pointed cone generated by prescribed rays, with exactly those extreme rays and with no additional prescribed ray inside it. Full-dimensional allowed blocks index the integer coefficient vector. Actual codimension-one allowed cones index the boundary matrix.

The main equivalence is

```lean
Unimodular.AllowedBlocks.theorem_3_1
```

For every integer coefficient vector `c` and every direction generic for the ray-generated hyperplanes, it identifies

$$
c\geq 0,\qquad \partial c=0,\qquad \lambda c=1
$$

with the existence of an actual complete fan having precisely the prescribed rays and maximal-cone indicator `c`. The theorem retains arbitrary rank and permits nonsimplicial and nonprojective fans. Its input consists of the equations; the face-intersection property and complete ray usage are conclusions of the proof.

Four companion results complete Theorem 1.1 and its immediate extensions:

| Lean declaration in `Unimodular.AllowedBlocks` | Conclusion |
|---|---|
| `theorem_3_1_zero_one` | Every solution coefficient is zero or one. |
| `theorem_1_1_connectivity` | Any two actual fans are joined by Graver-primitive integer moves, with all intermediate maximal cones drawn from the two endpoints. |
| `signed_degree_independent` | The degree of every signed integral boundary cycle is independent of the generic direction. |
| `theorem_3_1_any_orientation` | The characterization is independent of the chosen orientation convention. |

The definitions can be read in `AllowedBlocks.lean` and `FanGluing.lean`. The principal statements are in `MainFanTheorems.lean` and `FanConnectivity.lean`.

## Deep monotonicity and the exact minor class

An `OriginalSmoothReflexivePresentation` records a polytope in its original integral lattice, its primitive facet normals in the actual dual lattice, and the active-normal bases expressing smoothness. Its carrier is the actual convex hull, identified with its facet halfspaces. The constructor `ofActiveBases` obtains the presentation from the corresponding existence statements without requiring a preselected global coordinate basis.

Deep monotonicity means that, at every vertex, the entire real unit parallelepiped spanned by the actual primitive inward edge directions lies in the polytope. Coordinate transport preserves these whole sets. The normal matroid is the ordinary represented matroid of the geometric facet normals; changing integral coordinates preserves every independent subset.

The direct-sum assertion of Theorem 1.2 is

```lean
Unimodular.OriginalSmoothReflexivePresentation.theorem_1_2_original_lattice_direct_sum_networks
```

It decomposes the original normal matroid into its actual connected-component restrictions. Each component is connected and loopless and has an explicit eligible series-parallel network whose ordinary cycle matroid is the component after relabelling its full ground.

The exact minor-class assertion is

```lean
Unimodular.SeriesParallel.groundSeriesParallel_iff_normalMatroid_minor
```

It quantifies over every Mathlib matroid on an arbitrary label type. A matroid is series-parallel exactly when its actual-ground restriction embeds as an ordinary deletion/contraction minor of the primitive-normal matroid of a positive-dimensional deeply monotone smooth reflexive polytope. Loops and empty grounds are included. Unused ambient labels impose no additional finiteness hypothesis.

The constructive refinement `groundSeriesParallel_cubical_realization` gives dimension `m` for a nonempty series-parallel matroid with `m` ground elements. Its polytope is a combinatorial cube with `2m` facets and `2^m` vertices; its fan has `2m` rays and `2^m` maximal cones. The integral graphic realization also retains the original signed vectors and excludes an ordinary `K₄` graph minor. These results establish Theorem 6.6 and Proposition 6.7 as well.

Start with `OriginalPolytopeNetworks.lean`, `OriginalPolytopeMatroid.lean`, and `SeriesParallelClassification.lean`.
