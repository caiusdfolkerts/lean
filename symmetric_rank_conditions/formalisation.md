## The mathematical objects and their meaning

Lean uses indices $0,\ldots,n-1$. Independent symmetric entries are represented by

$$
\operatorname{Entry}(n)=\{(i,j)\in\operatorname{Fin}(n)^2:j\le i\},\qquad
\operatorname{fold}(i,j)=(\max(i,j),\min(i,j)).
$$

Write $S_n(K)=K[u]$ for the ordinary polynomial ring on these coordinates and $P_{n,t}(K)$ for the ideal of all $t$-minors; suppress $K$ when fixed. A monomial exponent is a finitely supported function $\operatorname{Entry}(n)\to\mathbb N$. Consequently folding preserves multiplicity: two transposed off-diagonal factors produce a square.

Increasing row and column lists are order embeddings $a,b:\operatorname{Fin}(t)\hookrightarrow\operatorname{Fin}(n)$. The polynomial `minor` is their actual matrix determinant. Its folded antidiagonal exponent is

$$
\sum_{p=0}^{t-1}\mathbf 1_{\operatorname{fold}(a(p),b(t-1-p))}.
$$

The prescribed graded reverse lexicographic order is `foldedOrder`. Folded pairs are ordered lexicographically as $(\max(i,j),\min(i,j))$; at a fixed total degree, the exponents are compared in reverse at the largest differing pair. This is the order of Marberg--Pawlowski, Remark 2.3, and the rank-ideal conclusions prove their Conjectures 6.5--6.7. The leading-term theorem computes the determinant's leading exponent and its sign; neither is built into the definition of a determinant.

For a symmetric nonnegative array $r$, $I_K(r)$ is generated by the prescribed northwest minors. Its geometric locus is defined independently by ranks of evaluated northwest submatrices. The proof relates rank bounds to minor vanishing before applying the algebraically closed radical--vanishing-ideal correspondence. In characteristic two a rank stratum may contain several congruence orbits; the formal statements concern the literal rank conditions.

The prime result retains independent realising and coefficient fields. The northwest Gröbner theorem permits every symmetric nonnegative bound array, including arrays that are not rank profiles. The mixed theorem permits every finite list $(t_\nu,s_\nu)$ with $1\le t_\nu\le n$ and $s_\nu\ge1$. The proposed symbolic generators are the literal products

$$
G_{n,t,s}=\left\{\prod_j\delta_j:
a_j=\operatorname{size}(\delta_j)\ge t,\quad
\sum_j(a_j-t+1)=s\right\}.
$$

The equality in this score condition is preserved.

Symbolic powers are defined through denominators outside the ideal. For prime $P$, the project proves equality with the contraction of $(P R_P)^s$ from the prime localisation. Thus the proposed generators are proved to generate an independently defined symbolic power. Over a general commutative coefficient ring, the integral conclusion concerns base change of the stated integral generators, as in the paper.

Integral dependence on an ideal also has its usual independent definition: $x$ is integral over $I$ if, for some $d>0$,

$$
x^d+c_{d-1}x^{d-1}+\cdots+c_0=0,
\qquad c_i\in I^{d-i}.
$$

`CommAlg.idealIntegralClosure` is proved to be an ideal and to agree with the degree-one integrality criterion in the actual Rees algebra. Transitivity and the power-root implication are proved from this definition.

These choices make the correspondence checks concrete: actual polynomial rings, determinants, ranks, localisation, ideal operations, convex hulls, and schemes occur in the final statements.

## Insertion, the carrier recurrence, and the core inductions

Theorem 2.1 is proved for the actual paired insertion algorithm. The formal development first handles matchings with distinct endpoint labels, then represents an arbitrary monomial by labelled occurrences. Repeated edges remain distinct occurrences, and a diagonal variable contributes two incidences. Standardisation and collapse preserve the order information needed for semistandard insertion.

The map is a content-preserving bijection onto all semistandard tableaux with even row lengths. If $\mu(m)$ halves those row lengths, write $\gamma_t(m)=\sum_{i\ge t}\mu_i(m)$, with one-based indices. The independently defined prefix-rank and packing statistics are proved equal to the tableau statistics, for every packing parameter and arbitrary multiplicities. Bounds making these maxima computable are proved chain-size bounds and impose no restriction on the monomial.

Matching dual equivalence supplies local chain and packing invariance. Connectivity and canonical nested matchings identify the prefix and shape statistics. The source then transports those results through the literal occurrence encoding. This proves both the statistics and the surjectivity and uniqueness needed later for counting.

Lemma 3.2 is a statement about the actual carrier algorithm. Deleting the first tableau row corresponds to a bijection of monomial exponents that separates the carrier variables from a smaller symmetric matrix. The development proves its inverse, its endpoint-weight identity, and its effect on every relevant rank and symbolic obstruction. The algebraic induction receives this proved interface.

The algebraic proof order is essential:

Northwest Gröbner bases are proved for arbitrary symmetric nonnegative arrays. Zero bounds are removed by the exact coordinate restriction; positive bounds use the diagonal Schur chart.

Ordinary symmetric determinantal primeness is proved before prime localisations are used in the symbolic argument.

The symbolic chart, polynomial-extension statements, and containment of the proposed products in genuine symbolic powers are established.

The mixed Gröbner induction uses the carrier correspondence and the comparison of actual homogeneous quotient pieces.

Realised-profile primeness is proved by the zero-row, diagonal-pivot, and off-diagonal-pivot cases.

For $U=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}x&b^{\mathsf T}\\b&C\end{smallmatrix}\right)$, put $D=C-x^{-1}bb^{\mathsf T}$. Over $F=K(x)$, for $t\ge2$, the equivalence `PaperStatements.lemma_5_2_quotient_chart` gives

$$
F[b,C]/P_{n,t}^{(s)}F[b,C]
\cong_F \bigl(F[D]/P_{n-1,t-1}^{(s)}F[D]\bigr)[b_2,\ldots,b_n].
$$

It composes the Schur automorphism with coefficientwise quotient maps; the carriers remain independent polynomial variables.

The comparison argument of Lemma 3.3 first works over the rational-function coefficient field $K(x)$. Equality of quotient dimensions identifies the initial ideal there. Division by the proposed basis then descends the equality: a nonzero remainder would retain a prohibited leading monomial after the injective coefficient extension.

Prime descent uses a different ring: it inverts only the regular pivot. The off-diagonal chart is a genuine invertible $2$-by-$2$ block calculation and does not divide by two. Its prefix identities distinguish rectangles containing zero, one, or two pivot indices. These facts preserve both characteristic two and all northwest conditions.

## Integral bases and one compatible deformation

The minor generators have leading coefficient $1$ or $-1$. After changing signs, monic integral division gives the same standard monomials over $\mathbb Z$ and after every commutative base change. The object `integralMixedBasis` is an actual module basis, and its application lemma identifies each vector with the indicated monomial residue class. The coefficient ring may have zero divisors.

For a realised rank profile, the quotient over a coefficient domain embeds into its extension to the fraction field. The primeness theorem over the fraction field then gives Corollary 6.1's domain statement. The geometric-fibre clause uses the explicit scalar-extension isomorphism

$$
S\otimes_R(R[u]/I_R(r))\cong_S S[u]/I_S(r).
$$

In particular, for $A=\mathbb Z[u]/I_{\mathbb Z}(r)$, the theorem concerns the literal ring $L\otimes_K(K\otimes_{\mathbb Z}A)$ for every field $K$ and every extension field $L$.

The simultaneous triangular basis of Lemma 6.2 is constructed degree by degree. Suppose the smaller monomials already have a common basis. For every ideal requiring the next monomial $m$, choose a normalised lift. Pairwise Gröbner compatibility forces the lifts' lower coefficients to agree at each basis index excluded from both ideals. Choosing those agreed coefficients produces a single new vector belonging to every required ideal. Induction proves both independence and the required span identities.

Only finitely many distinct rank and symbolic conditions affect a fixed degree. The degreewise constructions therefore give one basis for the whole family in Corollary 6.3. Each ideal is the span of a subset of that basis. Sums and intersections act by union and intersection of these subsets; this proves distributivity, the initial-ideal identities, and compatibility with arbitrary coefficient change. The quotient bases are the specified standard monomials.

For Proposition 6.4, the deformation of a minor is exactly

$$
\delta_\tau=\tau^{-\omega(A,B)}
\det(\tau^{ab}u_{ab})_{a\in A,b\in B},\qquad
\omega(A,B)=\sum_{p=1}^t a_p b_{t+1-p},
$$

with one-based indices in this displayed formula. Strict rearrangement proves that every exponent after normalisation is nonnegative. The code then proves the fibres at $\tau=1$ and $\tau=0$, and the diagonal rescaling when $\tau$ is invertible.

Monic division over $\mathbb Z[\tau]$ and the common triangular basis give free quotients for the entire lattice of finite sums and intersections. These are free over the actual parameter ring, hence flat. Arbitrary base change and specialisation commute with the lattice operations. The later integral-closure models are placed in this same lattice by an explicit finite symbolic-power expression.

The remark on other antidiagonal orders is proved over each commutative coefficient ring: selecting the same leading exponents gives the same Gröbner basis and standard-monomial basis. For the transpose-fixed-locus remark, the full-matrix coordinate ring modulo all $f-f^{\mathsf T}$ is proved isomorphic to the symmetric coordinate ring, without inverting two. The normalised minor families descend through this quotient. In size two the family is literally $\tau u_{11}u_{22}-u_{12}^2$, so its special fibre retains the square, after every coefficient change.

## Rank strata as schemes: Proposition 6.5

For a symmetric partial permutation $w$, the scheme $S_w$ is the open subscheme of the literal rank-locus quotient where all northwest ranks have their prescribed exact values. The construction uses vanishing of larger minors and the corresponding determinantal open conditions. The exact rank-$r$ condition over a ring is $I_r(A)=R$ and $I_{r+1}(A)=0$; its equivalence with ordinary matrix rank over a field is proved. The product description is an isomorphism of schemes over the coefficient base:

$$
S_w\cong \mathbb A^{a(w)}\times\mathbb G_m^{b(w)}.
$$

The product chart is represented by the polynomial coordinate ring with the product of its torus coordinates inverted. Its algebra maps are proved equivalent to choices of affine coordinates and genuine units.

Set $a(\varnothing)=b(\varnothing)=0$. In the paper's one-based indices, the recursion is:

| First row; indices removed           | Increase in $a$ | Increase in $b$ |
|:-------------------------------------|:----------------|:----------------|
| Zero; remove $1$                     | $0$             | $0$             |
| Rook at $(1,1)$; remove $1$          | $n-1$           | $1$             |
| Rook at $(1,k)$, $k>1$; remove $1,k$ | $2n-k-1$        | $1$             |

Each branch gives mutually inverse coordinate maps over an arbitrary commutative ring. The off-diagonal branch retains its free diagonal coordinate and the exact prefix-rank conditions. Recursive charts prove the exponents and their independence from auxiliary choices.

The scheme isomorphism is proved over the base, and the development applies the native pullback construction to obtain its version after an arbitrary scheme base change. This includes nonaffine base changes. The point-level coordinate descriptions are connected to the scheme maps through the affine functor of points.

Over a field, the stratum is nonempty and open. Primeness of the rank locus makes it dense. The formal geometry then establishes

$$
\dim X_w=a(w)+b(w),
$$

rationality and generic smoothness. Dimension is the ring Krull dimension of the actual coordinate quotient, also identified with the topological Krull dimension of the locus. The necessary finite-type dimension argument is proved from Noether normalisation and integral-extension dimension results; rationality and smoothness use Mathlib's scheme predicates and morphisms. Rationality is witnessed by affine space with exactly $a(w)+b(w)$ variables.

The counting argument independently classifies the northwest rank profile of every symmetric matrix over a field by a unique symmetric partial permutation. For a finite field $F$ this partitions the actual rank-bounded matrix locus into the product strata, yielding

$$
|X_w(F)|=\sum_{r_v\le r_w}|F|^{a(v)}(|F|-1)^{b(v)}.
$$

An additional bridge identifies these matrices with the $F$-rational points of the quotient scheme, with its coefficient map fixed. Thus the count agrees with the scheme-theoretic notation in the paper.

## Schur series and a basis of actual minor products

The endpoint multidegree of $u_{ij}$ is $e_i+e_j$. The project defines the Hilbert coefficient as the dimension of the corresponding component of the actual quotient. Standard-monomial bases identify this dimension with a finite count of exponents. The content-preserving insertion bijection then turns that count into the semistandard-tableau count.

Schur polynomials are represented by their semistandard-tableau generating functions. Their coefficients count semistandard tableaux of a fixed Young diagram and content. The development proves the relevant finiteness and the correspondence between row lists and Young-diagram tableaux. An explicit equivalence with Mathlib's `SemistandardYoungTableau`, restricted to entries less than $n$, identifies this definition with the usual finite-alphabet tableau sum. Equality of every coefficient gives equality in the multivariate formal power-series ring:

$$
\operatorname{Hilb}(S_n/P_{n,t}^{(s)};z_1,\ldots,z_n)
=\sum_{\substack{\ell(\mu)\le n\\\sum_{i\ge t}\mu_i<s}}
s_{2\mu}(z_1,\ldots,z_n).
$$

The series is locally finite coefficientwise. The rank-quotient formula, including the boundary cases of the row cutoff, follows from the same coefficient argument.

For the integral-closure application, more than a counting bijection is needed: one needs a basis whose vectors are actual products of minors. Given an even-row tableau $T$, pair its adjacent columns. Each pair has equal height and gives the row and column lists of a minor. Let $b_T$ be the product of these minors. Its degree and its complete symbolic-score vector are proved directly from the column counts.

Introduce an independent generic $n\times n$ matrix $Z=(z_{ij})$ and the polynomial map $\Phi:U\mapsto ZZ^{\mathsf T}$. Use lexicographic monomial order with variables prioritised by increasing (column, row) indices. For the image $\Phi([A\mid B])$ of a $t$-minor, the leading monomial is $\prod_{p=1}^t z_{a_p p}z_{b_p p}$, with coefficient one. The proof expands the determinant over all column-selection functions. Repeated selections vanish, and an explicit assignment comparison makes every other injective selection smaller.

For a product $b_T$, the distinguished exponent records the number of each label in each tableau row. These counts determine the tableau. Distinct tableaux consequently give distinct monic leading terms in Gram coordinates, proving linear independence over every field. Degreewise counting by the insertion bijection proves spanning. The resulting object `Gram.bitableauBasis` is a basis of the entire symmetric polynomial ring, with an application theorem identifying its actual minor products.

For example, in size two and degree two the six vectors may be written

$$
u_{11}^2,\quad u_{11}u_{12},\quad u_{11}u_{22},\quad
u_{12}u_{22},\quad u_{22}^2,\quad u_{11}u_{22}-u_{12}^2.
$$

The Gram leading-term argument applies unchanged in characteristic two. It is a polynomial calculation and requires no assertion that every symmetric matrix over the coefficient field admits a Gram factorisation.

## Integral closure and the polytope: Corollary 7.2

Fix a coefficient field $K$. Let $\Sigma$ be the paper's nonempty finite set of partitions into weakly decreasing positive parts, with largest part at most $n$. Put

$$
E(\Sigma)=\sum_{\sigma\in\Sigma}\prod_jP_{n,\sigma_j},\qquad
v_t(\sigma)=\sum_j\max(\sigma_j-t+1,0),\qquad
Q_\Sigma=\operatorname{conv}\{v(\sigma):\sigma\in\Sigma\}.
$$

The final wrapper uses the first-part bound literally; a separate lemma proves its equivalence with bounding every part. The main proof allows unordered size lists as well, and then specialises to partitions.

For the lower inclusion, a double Laplace expansion transfers size from a larger minor to a smaller one. For nonnested row sets, multiplying the exchange by the original product gives a monic quadratic ideal equation. Induction on row-set difference and the sum of squared minor sizes proves the integral-closure inclusion over the integers. A majorisation argument, with unit minors and size-one factors, handles arbitrary target products.

Elimination converts real convex feasibility to exact rational feasibility. Clearing denominators produces natural multiplicities of the target shapes. Determinant transfer places a power of $b_T$ in the integral closure of the corresponding ideal power; the Rees-algebra power-root lemma then proves that $b_T$ is integral over $E(\Sigma)$.

For the upper inclusion, assign each column $i$ of $Z$ cumulative weight $c_i=w_1+\cdots+w_i$, for arbitrary $w_t\ge0$. Every term of a Gram minor of size $a$ has at least the weight of the choice of the first $a$ columns, which attains all these bounds at once. Therefore

$$
\operatorname{minwt}_w(\Phi(b_T))=2\sum_{t=1}^n w_t\gamma_t(T).
$$

Here $\gamma_t(T)=\sum_j\max(a_j-t+1,0)$ for the sizes $a_j$ of the paired-column minors of $T$. The weights and the factor $2$ are real numbers; cancelling that positive factor imposes no invertibility condition on $2$ in the coefficient field. A leading-term argument proves the exact support statement for every linear combination in the bitableau basis: among the terms violating a proposed bound, the largest distinguished monomial cannot cancel. A monic integral equation cannot have one term of strictly smaller weight than all its other terms. At an excluded score vector $x$, separating the compact polytope $Q_\Sigma$ from the closed lower orthant $\{y:y\le x\}$ gives nonnegative separating weights. They force every basis element in the support of an integral element to have its score vector in $Q_\Sigma+\mathbb R_{\ge0}^n$.

These arguments prove the full closure formula, including its finiteness:

$$
\overline{E(\Sigma)}=
\sum_{a\in Q_\Sigma}\bigcap_{t=1}^nP_{n,t}^{(\lceil a_t\rceil)}.
$$

Only finitely many ceiling vectors occur. Exponent zero is represented by the whole ring. Symbolic-basis compatibility is also proved: the actual symbolic generators give the individual Gram-weight bounds, and each bitableau product belongs to the symbolic power indexed by its score. The Gram minor-product basis identifies the displayed expression with the closure. The earlier simultaneous integer triangular basis then supplies the folded-order initial identities, coefficient changes, and degenerations. Thus

$$
m\in\operatorname{in}\overline{E(\Sigma)}
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
(\gamma_1(m),\ldots,\gamma_n(m))\in Q_\Sigma+\mathbb R_{\ge0}^n.
$$

The same finite expression over $\mathbb Z$ supplies the integral model, free quotient bases under every commutative base change, and the flat family of Proposition 6.4 together with northwest ideals. It equals the actual integral closure after every field base change.

## Symbolic Rees algebras and the sharp SAGBI boundary

The symbolic Rees algebra is defined by actual coefficient membership in symbolic powers. Its SAGBI predicate includes algebra generation and generation of the initial subalgebra. Corollary 7.1 uses the order comparing the grading variable first, then the folded coefficient order. The basis of its initial algebra is indexed by every pair $(m,s)$ with $s\le\gamma_t(m)$, and its vector is literally $mT^s$.

For Proposition 7.3, let $D$ be the determinant and $A_n$ its full folded antidiagonal. The development proves, for all relevant indices and multiplicities,

$$
\gamma_r(mA_n^b)=\gamma_r(m)+b(n-r+1).
$$

This is derived from the actual carrier sweep. Added off-diagonal carrier amounts agree, so minimum commutes with their addition; the diagonal amount is even, so parity and division by two commute as well. Induction through the insertion recurrence gives the unrestricted identity.

The symbolic cancellation formula then follows by division by the genuine symbolic Gröbner basis. A nonzero standard remainder would have a score below the smaller threshold, while its determinant multiple would force the opposite inequality. Consequently, with $q=\max(a-b(n-r+1),0)$,

$$
D^bf\in P_{n,r}^{(a)}\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad f\in P_{n,r}^{(q)}.
$$

The code identifies $P_{n,n}^{(b)}=(D^b)$ and proves the actual intersection identity. Allocating the first symbolic degree among determinant factors and the remaining minor factors proves the positive joint SAGBI result.

The negative result concerns the actual joint algebra $\bigoplus_{a,b}(P_{n,2}^{(a)}\cap P_{n,t}^{(b)})T^aV^b$. The starting witness, in one-based indices, is

$$
m_4=u_{13}u_{23}u_{14}u_{24}u_{34}u_{15}.
$$

Its unique size-four antidiagonal divisor leaves two factors that do not form an antidiagonal, although it has a cover by two size-three antidiagonals. An inductive extension adds an extreme vertex and one edge at each step and proves the corresponding properties for every $t\ge4$ and every $n\ge t+1$.

For the extended witness, $\gamma_2(m_t)=t$ and $\gamma_t(m_t)=1$. Hence $m_tT^tV$ belongs to the true initial algebra. A proposed minor factorisation would have to supply $V$ using the unique size-$t$ antidiagonal. The remaining two occurrences cannot supply the missing $T$-degree. A support-closed subalgebra turns this obstruction to every finite factorisation into the failure of the actual SAGBI property.

Combining the positive and negative results proves the sharp boundary: for every field, $4\le t\le n$, and every permitted extension order, the natural collection $G_{2,t}$ is a SAGBI basis exactly when $n=t$.

The nonnormality example after Corollary 7.1 is also literal. For $n=4$ and $t=2$, put $m=u_{12}u_{34}$ and let $A$ be the initial algebra. Then $mT\notin A$, while $(mT)^2\in A$ and $mT=((u_{12}^2T)u_{34})/u_{12}$ is in $\operatorname{Frac}(A)$. The monic equation $X^2-(mT)^2$ therefore proves that $A$ fails Mathlib's native `IsIntegrallyClosed` predicate, over every field.
