# Rank formulas for homological shifts of polymatroidal ideals

The object being verified is the paper *Rank formulas for homological shifts of polymatroidal ideals*. The formalisation follows its mathematical content through actual polynomial ideals, actual derived Tor, and actual Rees algebras. Its central task is to prove that the partition formula describes the multidegrees of syzygies; the formula is never used as the definition of those syzygies.

## From polynomial ideals to a computable rank

Let $E$ be a finite set, let $k$ be any field, and write $S=k[x_j:j\in E]$. In Lean, the polynomial ring is `MvPolynomial E k`; exponent vectors are finitely supported functions $E\to\mathbb N$. A monomial ideal is an ordinary Mathlib `Ideal S`, generated by the specified coefficient-one monomials. The predicate saying that it is polymatroidal supplies an equigenerated finite exponent family satisfying the prescribed-donor exchange axiom. It assumes no resolution, rank formula, or homological conclusion.

A rank function $g$ is a nonnegative integral set function that is normalised, monotone, and submodular. Its base family is

$$
B(g)=\{a\in\mathbb N^E: a(E)=g(E),\quad a(A)\le g(A)\text{ for every }A\subseteq E\}.
$$

The formalisation proves that every nonempty polymatroidal generating family is exactly the base family of its canonical upper rank, and that every such rank has the expected bases. It also proves integral Minkowski decomposition. Consequently $I_gI_f=I_{g+f}$, and the powers appearing in the main theorems are genuine ideal powers, with $I_g^s=I_{sg}$ as a proved equality.

These statements account for the substantive discrete-polymatroid facts cited by the paper. They are established locally using a finite clone matroid and an integral submodular/supermodular sandwich argument. Thus the reference to an external theorem does not leave an axiom at the bottom of the proof.

## What "homological shift" means in the project

The paper defines

$$
\operatorname{HS}_i(I)=
\bigl(x^\alpha:\operatorname{Tor}_i^S(k,I)_\alpha\ne0\bigr).
$$

The formal definition retains this meaning. The polynomial variables form a regular sequence, and their exterior Koszul complex is constructed as a projective resolution of the quotient by all variables. The quotient is explicitly identified with $k$, viewed as an $S$-module through evaluation at zero. Mathlib's derived tensor functor `CategoryTheory.Tor'` is then identified with the homology computed from that resolution.

The coefficient Koszul complex has its actual monomial/exterior basis. A basis vector $x^a e_F$ has multidegree $a+\mathbf1_F$. The differential preserves this degree, so it restricts to homogeneous subcomplexes. Their homology inclusions define submodules of genuine derived Tor, and a checked internal-direct-sum theorem proves that these submodules exhaust Tor. Nonvanishing in the definition of the shift ideal is therefore nonvanishing of an actual homogeneous Tor component, rather than a combinatorial surrogate.

For a fixed exponent $\alpha$, the homogeneous complex is identified by an explicit signed chain isomorphism with the face complex

$$
\Delta_\alpha=\{F\subseteq\operatorname{supp}\alpha:
x^{\alpha-\mathbf1_F}\in I_g\}.
$$

The empty face is retained. A face of cardinality $i$ lies in Koszul degree $i$, corresponding to reduced simplicial degree $i-1$. This convention matters at homological degree zero and in rank-zero matroids.

The paper's lower membership inequalities identify $\Delta_\alpha$ as a matroid independence complex. The required constraint function is submodular but need not be monotone; the formal matroid construction preserves exactly that hypothesis. A deletion/contraction induction on the actual chain complexes proves concentration of independence-complex homology and its no-coloop nonvanishing criterion over every field. Linear quotients supply the separate vanishing outside the linear strand. Taken together, these results establish the full all-degree Tor support criterion.

## The partition formula and its consequences

On the active ground set, every nonempty subset has positive rank. Put

$$
D_g(A)=\min_{\pi\in\mathcal P(A)}\sum_{B\in\pi}(g(B)-1),
\qquad u_g(A)=|A|+D_g(A),
$$

with value zero at the empty set, and put

$$
h_{g,i}(A)=\min\{g(A)+i,u_g(A)\}\quad(A\ne\varnothing).
$$

Here $\mathcal P(A)$ consists of all partitions into nonempty blocks. Lean uses Mathlib's actual `Finpartition A`, so disjointness, nonempty blocks, and covering are built into the indexing object.

Uncrossing proves that $h_{g,i}$ is an integral polymatroid rank. The Koszul-matroid computation proves Theorem 1.2 in its two branches:

$$
\operatorname{HS}_i(I_g)=
\begin{cases}
I_{h_{g,i}},&h_{g,i}(E)=g(E)+i,\\
0,&h_{g,i}(E)<g(E)+i.
\end{cases}
$$

In the nonzero branch, maximising over the actual minimal monomial generators recovers $h_{g,i}(A)$ for every subset $A$. The implementation additionally handles inactive variables and zero rank, and proves agreement with the active-set formula; it does not interpret a negative inactive-block cost by silently truncating it.

For powers, define the nonnegative partition defect and slack by

$$
\delta_\rho(\pi)=\sum_{B\in\pi}\rho(B)-\rho(A),
\qquad b(\pi)=|A|-|\pi|.
$$

The rank of $\operatorname{HS}_i(I_\rho^s)$ is governed by the minimum of the affine expressions $s\delta_\rho(\pi)+b(\pi)$, truncated at $i$. Every positive slope is an integer at least one. Therefore, when $s\ge i$, it cannot affect the truncated minimum. Integral Minkowski decomposition then gives the ideal equality

$$
\operatorname{HS}_i(I^{s+1})=I\operatorname{HS}_i(I^s)
\qquad(s\ge i>0).
$$

The same argument retains a fixed rank summand for a twist $J$, and treats any finite number of independently powered factors. The squarefree Veronese ideal of degree $i$ on $i+1$ variables proves sharpness for every positive $i$.

The exact threshold analysis keeps all partitions, rather than only the uniform estimate. If $c_\rho(A)$ is the greatest number of blocks in a zero-defect partition, set

$$
\kappa_i(A)=\min\{i,|A|-c_\rho(A)\},\qquad
\tau_i(A)=\max\left(\{0\}\cup
\left\{\left\lceil\frac{\kappa_i(A)-b(\pi)}{\delta_\rho(\pi)}\right\rceil:
\delta_\rho(\pi)>0\right\}\right).
$$

The formalisation proves equality between its natural-valued implementation and this signed rational-ceiling formula, including negative numerators. It proves that the degrees of the actual minimal Rees-module generators are exactly the interval from $r_i=\tau_i(E)$ to $s_i=\max_{A\ne\varnothing}\tau_i(A)$. Every degree in the interval occurs, and $s_i$ is the least possible generation bound. If $i>|E|-c_\rho(E)$, the whole module vanishes.

Projective dimension is Mathlib's categorical projective dimension of the ideal as an $S$-module. It is not defined as the last nonzero Betti index. Generator exact sequences give the upper bound; actual Tor and a separately proved dimension-shifting argument give the lower bound. This proves the exact partition formula for $\operatorname{pd}_S(I^s)$ and its eventual value.

## Total support and integral closure

Writing $d=g(E)$ and $p=\operatorname{pd}_S(I_g)$, the actual ideal

$$
H(I)=\sum_{i=0}^{p}z^{p-i}\operatorname{HS}_i(I)\subseteq S[z]
$$

is proved polymatroidal and generated in degree $d+p$. On the active ground set, its canonical rank is

$$
F(A)=u_g(A),\qquad F(A\cup\{z\})=p+g(A).
$$

Its bases are exactly the pairs $(\alpha,p-i)$ with nonzero actual Tor component in degree $(i,\alpha)$. The support agrees over any two coefficient fields. The underlying statements also cover principal ideals, inactive variables, and the unit ideal; the displayed active-set formula is connected to that general implementation by a proved equality.

For integral closure, the ambient ordinary Rees algebra is Mathlib's actual subalgebra of $S[t]$. The multi-Rees construction is likewise a subalgebra of a polynomial ring, with coefficient membership in the corresponding products of ideal powers. The shift modules are proved to be ideals of these rings.

Integral dependence over an ideal is expressed by a monic equation

$$
x^n+\sum_{j<n}c_jx^j=0,\qquad n>0,\quad c_j\in M^{n-j}.
$$

Integer linear weight inequalities describe membership in the shift ideal. A lowest-weight noncancellation argument over a domain shows directly that a solution of such an equation satisfies every required inequality. This proves Theorem 6.1 and its twisted multi-Rees extension without assuming normality of the Rees algebra or importing an unproved valuation theorem.
