# Independent review of the paper-to-Lean correspondence

This review compares the eight release endpoints with the supplied manuscript, *The Hivert–Thiéry conjecture and differential normal forms*. It checks the mathematical statements and the definitions they use, separately from the compiler and kernel-dependency checks. The reviewed statements cover every printed clause of Theorems 1.1, 2.2, 6.2, and 6.3, and Proposition 6.5. The first two endpoints collect their respective results into single propositions; Theorem 6.3 and Proposition 6.5 have separate scalar, basis, or equivariant endpoints.

## Checks on definitions and hypotheses

The coinvariant operators are linear maps on actual multivariate polynomial rings. Their hit spaces are vector-space spans of all relevant operator images. They are not replaced by polynomial ideals, bounded-degree calculations, or a stipulated quotient dimension. The infinite tail is retained in the definitions and then proved equal to its finite presentation. The basis endpoints identify each vector with its stated monomial class. The representation endpoints supply genuine intertwiners and identify all homogeneous pieces over the original field; no splitting-field or separately assumed semisimplicity hypothesis has been added.

The independent lower bound in the weighted-tail proof uses actual polynomial families of finite complexes, exactness of their zero-slope Koszul fibre, and proved matrix-rank inequalities under specialization. The coordinate recursion retains the coefficient shifts caused by removing a variable and by extracting the product of all variables. The sharp converse in Theorem 1.1 uses the original operators and Fischer pairing, a constructed harmonic standard subrepresentation, its absence from the corresponding ordinary coinvariant degrees, and restriction to fewer variables. These arguments do not assume the desired basis or representation equivalence.

For Theorem 6.3, `IsolatedCriticalPointAtOrigin` has its ordinary local analytic meaning: the gradient vanishes at the origin, and the origin is its only zero in some neighbourhood. Positive weighted scaling proves the global common-zero statement, from which gradient regularity is derived. Regularity, torsion-freeness of multiplication by `F`, and freeness of the Brieskorn quotient are not extra hypotheses of the release endpoints.

The Brieskorn denominator is the vector-space sum of the actual Hamiltonian images `(F_j ∂_i − F_i ∂_j)(P)`, with `i < j`, as printed. The polynomial parameter action is identified with multiplication by `F`, and the coefficient ring is the literal subalgebra `Algebra.adjoin ℂ {F}`. The scalar and basis endpoints state the paper's freeness and arbitrary homogeneous-basis-lifting clauses. They do not separately export a global finite-rank formula or a named `Module.Finite` assertion over `ℂ[F]`.

The Hilbert-series coefficients are dimensions of the actual graded quotient pieces. `geometricInverse h` is proved to be the two-sided formal-power-series inverse of `1 − q^h`. The group actions are the usual pullback actions on polynomials, `p(x) ↦ p(g⁻¹x)`, and the matrix weight condition is proved equivalent to preservation of each coordinate's assigned degree. Naturality is proved for the actual quotient maps. The homogeneous equivariant splitting is constructed by averaging over the finite group.

For Proposition 6.5, the normalized coefficients are `h_a⁻¹ ∂_i F_a` and the target is the actual vector-valued polynomial module with basis degree `−h_a`. Its degree-`e` component has row `a` in polynomial degree `e+h_a`. The statement retains all `e : ℤ`, including negative target degrees. Each relevant piece is proved finite dimensional; the Hilbert identity does not rely on treating an infinite-dimensional whole quotient as having zero `finrank`.

The full-codimension Jacobian injectivity used in Proposition 6.5 is derived from the homogeneous regular sequence. The development proves finiteness over the actual parameter subalgebra and uses characteristic zero to establish injectivity. It does not assume a nonzero Jacobian determinant, algebraic independence, or the desired zero syzygy module in the final statement. The canonical defect map is identified explicitly with multiplication by the vector `(F₁,…,Fₙ)` followed by passage to the actual quotient.

## Scope and terminology

Theorem 6.3 computes the defect at every candidate Euler resonance `−h/(W+d)` with `P_d ≠ 0`; its formula permits that defect to be zero. Thus the list of candidate resonances should not be described as a list of parameters at which a nonzero jump always occurs. Proposition 6.5 does prove that stronger exact-exceptional-set assertion for its full-codimension family.

The declarations above establish the stated numbered results. The general differential normal-form theorems in §§3–5, including the noncommutative Theorem 5.1, and the intermediate-codimension complete-intersection Theorem 6.4 are outside these endpoints. Recovered auxiliary work for those sections is preserved separately. The package should therefore describe its coverage by the verified numbered results and the principal coinvariant theorems, rather than claim a formalisation of every result in the paper.

Within the eight reviewed endpoints, no conclusion-shaped premise, weaker replacement statement, bounded approximation, or missing printed clause was identified.
