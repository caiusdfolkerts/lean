# Hilbert series of exterior algebras modulo two quadrics

## What this development establishes

The development proves Theorem 1.1 in every dimension and all clauses of Theorem 1.3, including the actual graded flat degenerations. It also proves the exterior homology and Lie-cohomology calculations and the stated positive-characteristic results. It also checks substantial geometry of the actual common-isotropic schemes. The objects are exterior quotient algebras, schemes, sheaves, representations and cohomology complexes; numerical models are connected to those objects by proved maps.

**The complete-paper objective remains unfinished.** Theorem 1.2 for every nonempty regular zero scheme, the actual Chern-generated ring identifications in Theorem 4.1, and the sheaf-cohomology assertions of Section 6 are not all proved. The accepted project contains no assumed versions of these conclusions. A successful build certifies its stated theorems, not the missing claims.

For a field $K$ of characteristic zero and an $n$-dimensional vector space $M$, let $E=\bigwedge M$ and let $f,g\in\bigwedge^2M$. Taking $M=V^*$ gives the paper's convention. The full content of Theorem 1.1 is checked:

$$
\operatorname{Hilb}(E/(f,g),t)=1+\sum_{s=1}^{\lceil n/2\rceil}a(n,s)t^s,
\qquad (E/(f,g))_s=0\quad(s>\lceil n/2\rceil),
$$

for pairs in one nonempty Zariski-open set. If $w=n+2-2s$, then

$$
a(n,s)=\det\!\left[\binom{w+i-j}{j-i+1}\right]_{0\le i,j<s}.
$$

The same integer counts the paper's length-$n+2$ walks from $0$ to $w$, with steps $\pm1$ and heights in $[0,w]$. The conventions for out-of-range binomial coefficients and the empty determinant are retained.

Here "general" has explicit logical content. A basis of $\bigwedge^2M$ identifies all pairs with a full affine coefficient space. Lean constructs a polynomial $Q\ne0$, proves the existence of a point where $Q$ is nonzero, and proves the displayed conclusions for *every* pair with $Q\ne0$. The same $Q$ works in every degree. This is stronger than checking one normal-form pencil, or choosing a separate generic set for each degree.

The most convenient entry point is `genericHilbertSeriesAndVanishing`, in namespace\
`PaperVerification.MainStatements`. Its last clause says that every actual quotient element above the cutoff equals zero. It is not merely a convention assigning those degrees dimension zero. The original endpoints `paper_theorem_1_1` and `paper_theorem_1_1_hilbertSeries` remain available.

## From exterior algebra to the generic formula

The degree-$s$ calculation initially uses an explicit finite-dimensional presentation: the $s$th exterior power modulo the subspace generated by $f\wedge\eta$ and $g\wedge\eta$. This makes multiplication matrices and monomial bases accessible. A separate comparison identifies that presentation with the degree-$s$ piece of the actual quotient algebra

$$
\operatorname{ExteriorAlgebra}_K(M)/(f,g).
$$

The map `ExteriorAugmented.degreeEquivPiece` provides this bridge. Thus a rank or dimension theorem about the presentation is a theorem about the original quotient, with its original grading.

For the odd path pencil in dimension $2k+1$,

$$
F=\sum_{i=1}^k z_{2i-1}z_{2i},\qquad
G=\sum_{i=1}^k z_{2i}z_{2i+1},
$$

the checked basis consists of the original monomial classes

$$
z_{i_1}\cdots z_{i_s},\qquad
2j-1\le i_j\le 2k+1-2s+2j\quad(1\le j\le s).
$$

The basis theorem quantifies over every exterior degree, including the degrees where the indexing set is empty. It asserts the value of the basis map on every good index, so an arbitrary vector-space equivalence cannot stand in for the claimed monomials.

The proof chain includes the local straightening inverses, their integral denominators, spanning and independence, and the combinatorial identifications with confined walks and determinants. The generic result is obtained in the full coefficient space, with the necessary rank and specialization arguments proved in Lean. The checked algebraic route to Theorem 1.1 does not depend on an unproved invocation of the paper's sheaf-cohomology calculation.

### What an equivalence must preserve

Three recurrent comparisons matter throughout the project:

A quotient presentation is compared with the *actual* exterior algebra quotient and its homogeneous pieces.

A coordinate-ring presentation is compared with the original homogeneous Plücker ideal and its actual $\operatorname{Proj}$ scheme.

An auxiliary homology presentation is compared with the cohomology or Tor object built from the original differential or module action.

These comparisons are mathematical theorems, not changes of terminology. For example, a vector-space dimension identity cannot supply a ring isomorphism, and an arbitrary ring isomorphism cannot certify the named tautological Chern classes. The remaining geometric identifications are kept outside the completed claims for precisely this reason.

## Odd Plücker rings and their geometric meaning

For $1\le s\le k+1$, the paper studies the common-isotropic locus $X_s\subset\operatorname{Gr}(s,2k+1)$ and the interval of $s$-subsets

$$
\alpha=(1,3,\ldots,2s-1),\qquad
\beta=(2k+3-2s,2k+5-2s,\ldots,2k+1).
$$

The accepted algebraic core constructs the literal weighted Rees image, proves flatness over the polynomial parameter ring, and identifies its special and nonzero fibers. It also proves the Hibi degeneration, the quadratic Gröbner presentation in good Plücker coordinates, and homological Koszulness. The complete twisting-section ring is identified with the original graded ring, in all nonnegative degrees.

### The zero scheme is intrinsic

The ambient comparison constructs the genuine tautological subbundle and the alternating restrictions to it. The full image ideal of these sections is proved equal to the kernel of the original Plücker closed immersion. Consequently `isotropicIntrinsicZeroIso` identifies the original scheme with the intrinsic common-isotropic zero scheme. The equality is of ideal sheaves, without passing to radicals or merely comparing field-valued points.

This construction works over every field, for any finite tuple of alternating forms and every rank at least two. A separate rank-one branch proves that the actual second exterior-power sheaf of the tautological line is zero. Isotropy therefore imposes no equation in that branch. Together they cover the paper's full positive range, including $k=0$.

### The target really is the Richardson rank locus

The opposite-flag conditions are defined independently by minors of actual tautological coordinate projections. Determinant expansion and pivot identities identify the corresponding ideal with the forbidden Plücker coordinates outside $[\alpha,\beta]$. The theorem `richardsonRankZeroIso` is a scheme isomorphism over the ambient Grassmannian. The coordinate-ring map preserves the original polynomial classes and each ordinary homogeneous piece.

Both geometric comparisons preserve the Plücker polarization and every integer twist, including negative twists. Their hypotheses introduce no smoothness, normality or characteristic-zero assumption. They therefore apply to the characteristic ranges of Section 7 as well.

**Theorem 1.3 is complete.** The accepted grading continuation constructs the genuine $K[t]$ grading of both literal Rees families, with $t$ in degree zero. The fiber gradings are induced by the homogeneous $(t-c)$ quotients, and the existing fiber isomorphisms identify every ordinary homogeneous piece with the original endpoint ring. ## Bigrading, Koszulness, Tor and Lie cohomology

The Section 5 results retain the algebraic structures appearing in the paper. Proposition 5.1 gives the entire bigraded Hilbert function of the odd pencil, with the transfer-matrix recurrence for $D_m$. Corollary 5.2 proves an equivalence of the actual complex $\mathrm{SL}_2$ representations

$$
A_{p,q}\simeq\operatorname{Sym}^{2p+1}(\operatorname{Sym}^q U),\qquad p+q=k,
$$

as well as $\dim A_{p,q}=\binom{2p+q+1}{q}$. Character equality is connected to a representation equivalence; the final assertion is not merely a matching list of weights.

Proposition 5.3 is stated using intrinsic graded Koszulness. The checked comparison with ordinary Tor ensures that a convenient resolution certificate is equivalent to that property, rather than defining a weaker substitute for it. For general pairs, the result is exactly $A_n$ Koszul if and only if $n\le4$. The quadratic-dual/enveloping-algebra statement and its Hilbert series hold in the printed range $n\ge6$.

### The two-generator action

For arbitrary $f,g$, give $E=\bigwedge M$ the $K[u,v]$-module structure $u\cdot x=fx$, $v\cdot x=gx$, with $\deg u=\deg v=2$. Writing $H(t)=\operatorname{Hilb}(E/(f,g),t)$, the actual graded Tor polynomials are

$$
\begin{align*}
B_0(t)&=H(t),&B_2(t)&=t^{n+4}H(t^{-1}),\\
B_1(t)&=H(t)+t^{n+4}H(t^{-1})-(1-t^2)^2(1+t)^n.
\end{align*}
$$

There are no later terms. The proof compares the two-generator Koszul complex with ordinary Tor over the genuine polynomial ring. The perfect exterior pairing identifies the annihilator term with the dual quotient; the middle polynomial follows from the actual Euler characteristic.

### The original two-step Lie algebra

The extension after Proposition 5.4 is also checked. Its Lie algebra is literally $V\oplus K^2$, with bracket

$$
[(x,a),(y,b)]=(0,f(x,y),g(x,y)).
$$

Both central coordinates remain present even for dependent or zero forms. The cochains are actual exterior powers of the dual. The constructed square-zero odd derivation is proved to be the unique differential whose value on a one-cochain is the negative bracket dual. Its categorical cohomology is then compared with the folded Koszul complex.

For every field and arbitrary forms, this gives

$$
\sum_j\dim\operatorname{H}^j(\mathfrak n_{f,g};K)t^j
=H(t)+t^{-1}B_1(t)+t^{n+2}H(t^{-1}),\qquad
\sum_j\dim\operatorname{H}^j(\mathfrak n_{f,g};K)=4H(1).
$$

The apparent Laurent expression is proved to be an ordinary polynomial. The degreewise theorem quantifies over every integer, including negative degrees. The main endpoints are `paper_lie_cohomology_poincare` and `total_dimension_four`, in namespace\
`PaperVerification.TwoStepOriginalLieCohomology`.

## Characteristic and integral base change

The characteristic assertions are checked with their full printed ranges. Let $K$ have prime characteristic $p$, and retain the canonical odd path pencil in dimension $n=2k+1$. If

$$
s<2p,\qquad s\le k+1,
$$

the good monomials form a basis in degree $s$, and the original Plücker ideal has the stated Richardson initial ideal. In particular, the full characteristic-zero Hilbert series holds when $n\le4p-3$. The boundary case $s=1$ is checked separately and connected to the same geometric objects; it is not excluded by a rank-at-least-two implementation.

The graded-family continuation also proves that the ordinary gradings and every fiber comparison survive in this range, including characteristic two. The rank-one family works over every field.

The high-degree vanishing needed for the full Hilbert series is proved from annihilation of the top good monomial. Thus the finite range of basis theorems is supplemented by an actual algebraic vanishing argument, not by truncating the formal series by definition.

### The integral assertion is a basis theorem

Put $M_0=\lfloor(k+1)/2\rfloor$ and assign independent invertible coefficients to the successive path edges. Over the actual ring

$$
\mathbb Z[1/M_0!][c_1^{\pm1},\ldots,c_{2k}^{\pm1}],
$$

the original good monomial classes are proved to form a free basis of the full exterior quotient. The local denominators are controlled, and a diagonal change of generators removes the edge coefficients along the path. The result survives arbitrary scalar-ring homomorphisms from this ring.

The formal endpoints construct the basis and prove its value on every specified monomial. They also construct the scalar-extension comparison and its basis formula. Consequently the base-change statement applies to arbitrary target rings admitting the required homomorphism; it does not silently restrict to fields, characteristic zero, or flat base change.

## The precise boundary in Section 4

For $n=2k+1$, put $w=2k+3-2s$, $c=w-1$, and $D=sc$. The algebraic quotient appearing in Theorem 4.1 is fully checked:

$$
B=\mathbb Q[c_1,\ldots,c_s]/(R_w,\ldots,R_{w+s-1}),\qquad \deg c_i=i,
$$

where, in symmetric roots $x_1,\ldots,x_s$,

$$
R_{2m}=h_m(x_1^2,\ldots,x_s^2),\quad
R_{2m+1}=\sum_i x_i
\frac{\partial h_{m+1}}{\partial y_i}(x_1^2,\ldots,x_s^2).
$$

The relations form a homogeneous regular sequence, and

$$
\operatorname{Hilb}(B,q)=\prod_{i=1}^s\frac{1-q^{c+i}}{1-q^i}
=\genfrac{[}{]}{0pt}{}{2k+2-s}{s}_q.
$$

The coefficient functional

$$
L(P)=\frac{(-1)^{\binom s2}}{s!}
[x_1^{2k}\cdots x_s^{2k}]\,P(x)\,\Delta(x_1^2,\ldots,x_s^2)^2
$$

has exactly the asserted relation ideal as the radical of its multiplication pairing. These are theorems about the literal polynomial quotient.

### Actual geometry already checked

The odd common-isotropic scheme is proved nonempty, smooth in characteristic zero, of dimension $D$, and rational throughout $1\le s\le k+1$. The rationality proof supplies a dense affine chart and actual scheme maps. For $s\ge2$, a genuine affine paving has been checked: the original normalized Plücker chart quotients are reduced by triangular elimination, forbidden charts are empty, and the allowed cells are isomorphic over the base field to affine spaces. The successive closed/open complements are proved equal as geometric loci. The exact cell count is $\binom{2k+2-s}{s}$.

Actual Chow localization now gives

$$
\dim_{\mathbb Q}\!\left(\bigoplus_{d\ge0}\operatorname{CH}_d(X_s)\otimes\mathbb Q\right)
\le \binom{2k+2-s}{s}.
$$

Finiteness is proved separately from the bound. The genuine rational-equivalence quotient is used in every degree, including the one-plane boundary and $k=0$.

### What is still required for the ring theorem

An isomorphism with $\operatorname{CH}^*(X_s)\otimes\mathbb Q$ requires an evaluation map into the actual Chow ring taking $c_i$ to the actual tautological Chern class, together with the geometric trace formula $\int_{X_s}P=L(P)$. That trace entails the Grassmannian integration formula and the regular-zero Euler-class identity for the two alternating sections. These geometric identifications are not yet checked.

Similarly, the ordinary singular-cohomology formulation needs a genuine multiplicative cycle-class comparison with Chern compatibility, or a direct topological Chern evaluation and integration argument. A chosen linear isomorphism between equal-dimensional spaces would not suffice. The localization and Thom continuations concern actual groups, but they do not by themselves prove multiplication or identify the generators.

The geometric ring presentation, the exact graded Betti polynomial, and odd-cohomology vanishing remain unproved. Rationality and the algebraic regular-sequence statement are checked.

## The remaining general sheaf-cohomology argument

Theorem 1.2 quantifies over every nonempty regular zero scheme $X_s\subset\operatorname{Gr}(s,n)$ when $n\ge2s-1$. For all $r\ge0$, it asserts

$$
h^0(X_s,\mathcal O_{X_s}(r))=
\det\!\left[\binom{w+r-1+i-j}{r-i+j}\right]_{0\le i,j<s},
\qquad \operatorname{H}^{>0}(X_s,\mathcal O_{X_s}(r))=0.
$$

It also asserts generation in degree one, the stated ideal, Cohen--Macaulay and Gorenstein properties, $a$-invariant $-w$, and projective normality under the paper's normality hypothesis. The odd canonical-pencil calculation cannot substitute for these quantifiers.

The central missing chain passes through the actual homogeneous bundles

$$
K_j(r)=\bigwedge^j\!\left(\bigwedge^2\mathcal U\otimes W\right)
\otimes\mathcal O_{\operatorname{Gr}}(r),\qquad \dim W=2.
$$

One needs their actual Schur constituents, the Borel--Weil--Bott cohomology calculation with its precise weights, and the resulting comparison of the sheaf Koszul complex with global sections. Numerical colored-edge inequalities constrain those weights, but do not alone construct this representation decomposition or compute sheaf cohomology.

The accepted supporting development includes actual relative projectivization, local universal quotient and kernel frames, the natural Čech--Leray comparison for finite Leray covers, and comparison of the original Ext cohomology with right-derived global sections. Every accepted component is listed in the project manifest. Imported source availability and native-only draft checks are not counted as completed mathematical dependencies.

### Several alternating forms

Section 6 replaces the two-dimensional space $W$ by one of dimension $m\ge3$, assumes the sheaf Koszul complex is exact, and sets

$$
\iota=n-m(s-1),\qquad D=s(n-s)-m\binom s2.
$$

Theorem 6.1 compares the degree-$s$ exterior Koszul complex with $\operatorname{H}^0(X,\mathcal O_X(1))$ when $\iota\ge0$, and proves higher cohomology vanishing. Proposition 6.2 distinguishes $\iota>0$ from the Calabi--Yau boundary $\iota=0$, including its zero-dimensional case.

The numerical incidence estimates and generic smoothness ingredients do not complete these statements. The actual Schur/cohomology comparison, spectral-sequence conclusions and adjunction applications remain to be verified. No additional hypothesis asserting their conclusions is inserted in a theorem advertised as the paper's result.

These are gaps in the formalisation, not refutations of the paper’s theorems. The included PDF retains the original statements.
