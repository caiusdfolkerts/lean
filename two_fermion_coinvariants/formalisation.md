# Monomial bases for two-fermion coinvariants

## The mathematical objects and the scope

This note explains how the principal results of *Monomial bases for two-fermion coinvariants* are represented in Lean 4. The code keeps the original quotient algebras, monomial families, gradings, group actions and coefficient hypotheses. A basis is proved by linear independence and spanning of the specified residue classes. A Hilbert coefficient is the rank of an independently defined homogeneous piece.

The correspondence is organised around the following statements. Section numbers and equation numbers refer to the paper supplied with the project.

| Paper | Formal mathematical content |
|:---|:---|
| Theorems 1.1, 1.2 | Paired and strict monomial bases over arbitrary allowed coefficient rings; ranks, exterior Hilbert polynomials and associated graded algebras; the finite free paired homogenisation. |
| Proposition 2.1 | Rectangular monomial bases of the original polynomial colon quotients for every monic $G$, with regular generators and the stated highest-form degrees. |
| Proposition 7.1 | The exact subset-sum criterion for finiteness of separated paired and strict moment quotients. |
| Theorem 7.2 | The actual bigraded local algebras, their signed tensor factors and the global product decomposition, with local ranks and exterior Hilbert coefficients. |
| Section 8.1 | Equality of invariant-generated and moment ideals for the stated wreath-product actions; literal bases and full polynomial/exterior Hilbert formulas, including the type-A and type-B identifications. |
| Theorem 8.1; (22), (23) | The smallest-weight initial ideal and equivariant Rees degeneration; polynomially graded and Eulerian color characters; the actual $\operatorname{GL}_2$-module and irreducible Schur decompositions. |

For a commutative ring $K$, the ambient algebra is literally

$$
P=K[x_1,\ldots,x_n]\otimes_K
\Lambda_K(K^n\oplus K^n).
$$

The two summands have generators $\theta_i$ and $\xi_i$. In Lean this is `SuperRing K n`: a tensor product of `MvPolynomial` and `ExteriorAlgebra`. The moment ideals are two-sided ideals in this algebra. Thus the exterior signs and square-zero relations are present in the quotient from the outset.

The paired ideal is generated by all four families, for every $k\geq0$,

$$
\begin{aligned}
P_k&=\sum_i\beta_i a_i b_i\phi_i^k,&
Q_k^\theta&=\sum_i b_i\phi_i^k\theta_i,\\
Q_k^\xi&=\sum_i\beta_i a_i\phi_i^k\xi_i,&
E_k&=\sum_i\phi_i^k\theta_i\xi_i.
\end{aligned}
$$

The finite presentation used in the proofs is derived from these infinite families. It is not an alternative definition that discards higher moments.

## The two basis theorems

The parameter structure `PairedData` records exactly the hypotheses of Theorem 1.1: $n,s\geq1$, $r_i>0$, $u_i,v_i\geq0$, $u_i+v_i=r_i s$, and

$$
n!\in K^\times,\qquad
\sum_{i\in U}\beta_i\in K^\times
\quad(\varnothing\ne U\subseteq[n]).
$$

The polynomials $\phi_i,a_i,b_i$ may depend on every variable. With $\mathrm{wt}(x_i)=1/r_i$, their highest forms are respectively $x_i^{r_i},x_i^{u_i},x_i^{v_i}$, with coefficient one. The definition `HasHighestPower` spells this out coefficient by coefficient: every other supported monomial has strictly smaller rational weight.

For exterior supports $T,S$, let

$$
t_i=\mathbf1_{i\in T},\quad z_i=\mathbf1_{i\in S},\quad
m_i=t_i+z_i,\quad c_i=i-\sum_{j\leq i}m_j,\quad c_0=0.
$$

The index type `PairedIndex d` consists of precisely the triples $(T,S,e)$ satisfying

$$
c_i\geq0,\qquad
0\leq e_i<d_i:=r_i(s+c_{i-1})-u_i t_i-v_i z_i
\quad\text{for every }i.
$$

The bound is an integer. A nonpositive bound makes the corresponding box empty; no truncation silently changes the inequality. The vector indexed by $(T,S,e)$ is the class of $x^e\theta_T\xi_S$, ordered increasingly within each color, with all theta variables before the xi variables.

The theorem `theorem_1_1` proves that this family is a free basis and also proves

$$
\operatorname{rank}_K(P/I)=2^n\Bigl(\prod_i r_i\Bigr)(s)_{n},\qquad
\sum_j\operatorname{rank}_K(P/I)_{\mathrm{ext}=j}z^j
=\Bigl(\prod_i r_i\Bigr)(s)_{n}(1+z)^n.
$$

Here $(s)_{n}=s(s+1)\cdots(s+n-1)$, represented by `Nat.ascFactorial`. The exterior-degree pieces are images of the original homogeneous exterior spaces under the quotient map.

Give $\theta_i$ weight $u_i/r_i$ and $\xi_i$ weight $v_i/r_i$. The associated graded algebra of the actual quotient filtration is proved isomorphic to the homogeneous power quotient. Clearing the denominators only rescales the grading; the rational and integral weight inequalities are proved equivalent. Weighted homogenisation supplies a finite free, hence flat, $K[t]$-algebra with the stated basis and the actual algebra fibers at $t=0$ and $t=1$.

For the strict ideal, the ordinary moments start at $k=1$; both odd families and the mixed family start at $k=0$. The structure `StrictData` requires only the highest forms of the $\phi_i$. Theorem `theorem_1_2` gives exactly

$$
\{x^e\theta_T\xi_S:c_i\geq1,\ 0\leq e_i<r_i c_i\},
$$

with rank $2^{n-1}n!\prod_i r_i$, exterior polynomial $n!\prod_i r_i(1+z)^{n-1}$, and the strict power algebra as its associated graded, with exterior weight zero.

These assertions include the zero coefficient ring. Their all-ring rank meaning is an explicit isomorphism with $K^N$, supplied by `theorem_1_1_free_rank` and `theorem_1_2_free_rank`. The separate intrinsic `Module.finrank` formulas assume `Nontrivial K`; that convention does not restrict either basis theorem.

## The proof dependencies

The proof has three interacting parts: polynomial colon quotients, exterior relations, and passage from homogeneous field coefficients to the full coefficient-ring statement. The formalisation develops the missing arguments explicitly and uses Mathlib for the underlying polynomial, exterior, localization, module and graded-algebra constructions.

**Polynomial boxes.** The rational Newton fields are constructed in the fraction field of the polynomial ring. Their divisibility and pole conditions are proved, as are the deletion identities and determinant nonvanishing. These results give regular sequences and rectangular monomial bases for the polynomial colon quotients needed by each exterior pattern. The degrees are the signed prefix heights. The argument covers every number of variables and every admissible profile used in the main theorems.

The separate endpoint `Newton.proposition_2_1` retains an arbitrary monic polynomial $G$ of positive degree, rather than only $G=t^s$. It proves the rectangular basis of the actual quotient $F[y_1,\ldots,y_n]/(J_{G,\beta}:g_\sigma)$, equality of that colon ideal with the ideal of the explicit contraction generators, regularity of those generators, and nonvanishing of their highest forms in degrees $h_i=s+\sum_{j<i}\sigma_j>0$. The monic proof lifts the homogeneous contractions through the total-degree filtration; the comparison determinant identifies the original colon ideal. No splitting-field hypothesis is added.

These complete-intersection calculations supply a Frobenius pairing on the actual ordinary quotient. Its dimension and nondegeneracy are proved; neither is added to the input data.

**Exterior independence and spanning.** Creation operators pair triangularly with the polynomial boxes. Their leading exterior terms are the specified ordered monomials, so the pairing proves independence of the literal family in the statement. On the other side, the exterior Gauss identities impose a bound at every prefix, including prefixes of a word that has an arbitrary colored suffix. The endpoint colon calculations give the matching upper bounds. Equal dimensions then prove that the proposed classes span over the field.

This separation matters under perturbation. Highest-form reduction by itself proves spanning but allows dimensions to drop. The filtered regular-sequence argument and the triangular Frobenius pairing preserve the required independence when arbitrary coupled lower-weight terms are introduced. The proof therefore retains the full hypothesis (2) of the paper, including mixed lower terms between different polynomial variables.

**Descent to arbitrary rings.** A universal coefficient ring records the finitely many lower coefficients and inverts the factorial and subset sums. The argument proves the basis on every residue-field fiber and descends it over the universal ring; base change then gives the result for every allowed commutative ring, including nonreduced rings. The finite presentation of the moment ideal is used to control the quotient throughout this passage. The universal construction supplies proofs, rather than placing a flatness or basis assumption in `PairedData` or `StrictData`.

The strict case has its own literal moment ideal and prefix inequalities. Its relation to the paired calculation removes one free exterior direction and accounts for the change from $2^n(s)_{n}$ to $2^{n-1}n!$. The path enumerations are proved for arbitrary length; they determine every exterior Hilbert coefficient, not only the total rank.

**The dependency record.** The dependency record follows actual proof bodies to pinned Lean and Mathlib results. The vendored TauCeti arguments and ported Eulerian proofs are included in that traversal. An asserted project theorem cannot pass merely because its name is available as an import.

## Finiteness and the local factors

For separated monic covers $a_i b_i=G(\phi_i)$, Proposition 7.1 is an equivalence: the full quotient is finite over the coefficient ring if and only if every nonempty subset sum of the weights is a unit. The formal proof establishes this for both the paired and strict families. It proves the necessity using a residue-field fiber and a nonconstant line on which the ordinary moments vanish. The monic cover and the ordinary part of the superalgebra are related to the original quotient by proved maps, so finiteness is not assumed in order to prove the criterion.

For the local theorem, work over an algebraically closed field of characteristic zero; this includes the paper's field $\mathbb C$. A proved factorisation lemma shows that factors of a nonzero univariate polynomial are univariate over a field. It identifies the separated input format with the original monic-cover data, preserving the polynomials and the literal moment ideal. Let $A$ be the ordinary moment quotient. A support point is literally a tuple $\rho=(\rho_i)$ with

$$
G(\phi_i(\rho_i))=0\qquad\text{for every }i.
$$

An equivalence with the maximal ideals of $A$ proves that all local factors have been included. The source local superalgebra is $A_{\mathfrak m_\rho}\otimes_A(P/I)$, so its nilpotents are retained.

Partition the indices by $\lambda=\phi_i(\rho_i)$. The parameters of the factor on $C_\lambda$ are the actual multiplicities

$$
s_\lambda=\operatorname{ord}_\lambda G,\quad
r'_i=\operatorname{ord}_{\rho_i}(\phi_i-\lambda),\quad
u'_i=\operatorname{ord}_{\rho_i}a_i,\quad
v'_i=\operatorname{ord}_{\rho_i}b_i.
$$

The identity $u'_i+v'_i=r'_i s_\lambda$, positivity where required, and the inherited subset-sum conditions are derived from the original data. No local basis or proposed isomorphism is a field of the input structure `SeparatedPairedData`.

The proof constructs a ramified formal coordinate and its inverse, then evaluates them on nilpotents through finite truncations. Hermite interpolation separates the moment families cluster by cluster and removes the unit factors after rescaling the odd coordinates. The independent cluster presentations are identified with their signed tensor product by universal properties. Odd elements in different factors anticommute. The separate theta and xi gradings are defined on both sides before the equivalence is proved to preserve them.

The endpoint `theorem_7_2` packages the bigraded local equivalences, the finite product decomposition and the fact that every enumerated local factor is nonzero. With $n_\lambda=|C_\lambda|$, the subsequent rank and Hilbert theorems give

$$
\begin{split}
\dim (P/I)_\rho
&=2^n\Bigl(\prod_i r'_i\Bigr)
\prod_\lambda(s_\lambda)_{n_\lambda},\\
\operatorname{Hilb}_{\mathrm{ext}}((P/I)_\rho;z)
&=\Bigl(\prod_i r'_i\Bigr)
\prod_\lambda(s_\lambda)_{n_\lambda}(1+z)^n.
\end{split}
$$

The global dimension is the sum over the entire support. The theorem `localRank_sum_closed` identifies this sum with $2^n(\prod_i\deg\phi_i)(\deg G)_n$. The endpoint `theorem_7_2_complex` specialises the decomposition to the paper's field $\mathbb C$. Repeated roots, ramified covers and zero orders of one of the two prefactors are all included.

## The invariant-theoretic applications

For $r\geq2$ and $1\leq a\leq r-1$, the source defines the action of $\mu_r^n\rtimes S_n$ on the original polynomial/exterior algebra. The cyclic characters on $x_i,\theta_i,\xi_i$ are $1,a,-a$, and permutations act on the indices with the exterior signs that this entails. The code's exponent $r-a$ is proved equal to the integer character $-a$ on the roots-of-unity group, including the full wreath-action formula on $\xi_i$. A positive invariant is defined by invariance and zero augmentation. The positive invariant ideal is the two-sided ideal they generate, independently of the moment presentation.

The Reynolds argument then proves that this ideal equals the paired moment ideal. The diagonal eigenvalue equations restrict every nonzero monomial coefficient to the permitted one-index blocks; induction on the number of occupied indices expresses the remaining orbit sums in the block sums. The formal proof keeps the shuffle signs and discharges the required integer divisions from the factorial-unit hypothesis. With $s=1$, $r_i=r$, $u_i=a$ and $v_i=r-a$, the main theorem gives

$$
x^e\theta_T\xi_S,\qquad c_i\geq0,\qquad
0\leq e_i<r c_i+(r-a)t_i+a z_i.
$$

This is equation (20), in the actual invariant quotient. Its dimension and exterior Hilbert polynomial are

$$
(2r)^n n!,\qquad r^n n!(1+z)^n.
$$

The full series is also identified coefficient by coefficient:

$$
\operatorname{Hilb}(q,u,v)=\sum_{T,S:\ c_i\geq0\ \forall i}
u^{|T|}v^{|S|}\prod_i[d_i]_q,
\qquad [d]_q=1+q+\cdots+q^{d-1}.
$$

The three degree spaces are images of ambient monomials of polynomial degree $k$, theta degree $p$ and xi degree $h$. A grading on the quotient and its homogeneous basis are proved from the established ideal equality. This avoids defining the degree spaces from the proposed answer.

When both exterior characters are trivial, a separate invariant-ideal proof gives the strict quotient with $\phi_i=x_i^r$. Its basis has $c_i\geq1$, $0\leq e_i<r c_i$, and dimension $r^n2^{n-1}n!$. Its full Hilbert coefficients are proved in the same physical grading. The type-A result is available over every commutative ring in which $n!$ is a unit, in particular over $\mathbb Z[1/n!]$. The field versions cover the paper's positive-characteristic hypotheses: characteristic greater than $n$, a primitive $r$-th root, and invertible $r$. The supporting lemma `primitive_root_order_unit` proves that the primitive-root hypothesis already implies the last condition for positive $r$. No restriction of this kind is added to the covering degrees in the general moment theorems.

**Identification with Lentfer's bounds.** For type A, the upper endpoint is $c_i-1$, with initial value zero and increments $1-m_i$. For type B, specialise $r=2,a=1$; the upper endpoint is $b_i=2c_i+m_i-1$, so

$$
b_1=1-m_1,\qquad b_i-b_{i-1}=2-m_i-m_{i-1}.
$$

The initial conditions, recurrences and their uniqueness are proved. They give the exact correspondence with Definitions 3.1 and 9.5 of Lentfer's paper, including every prefix inequality. The exterior word orders used in the two presentations differ by explicit units of sign $\pm1$. The general ordered-word comparison proves that this change of order preserves the stated monomial basis.

## The color filtration and the representation formulas

In type A let $J=(p_1,\ldots,p_n)\subseteq A=\mathbb C[x_1,\ldots,x_n]$. For $m\in\{0,1,2\}^n$, put $c_i=i-\sum_{j\leq i}m_j$ and define

$$
f_m=\prod_i\Bigl(x_i\prod_{j>i}(x_i-x_j)\Bigr)^{m_i},
\qquad K_m=J:f_m,\qquad C_m=A/K_m.
$$

Admissibility means $c_i\geq1$ at every prefix. The coefficient ideals and their polynomial degree pieces are defined from these formulas. The filtration gives both exterior colors at index $i$ weight $3^{i-1}$, and every polynomial variable weight zero.

The theorem `theorem_8_1` proves the smallest-weight initial-ideal identity (21), including the vanishing of inadmissible blocks. It identifies the actual associated graded with

$$
\bigoplus_{m\ \mathrm{admissible}}
C_m\otimes\bigotimes_i\Lambda^{m_i}V,
\qquad V=\mathbb C^2.
$$

Its multiplication is specified on a proved spanning family by the original polynomial and exterior products. The Rees algebra is defined as the corresponding subalgebra of Laurent polynomials; membership is proved equivalent to its coefficient filtration conditions. It is finite free over $\mathbb C[t]$, and its fibers at zero and one are the associated graded and original quotient. Every nonzero fiber is proved algebra-isomorphic to the original quotient by scaling each basis vector by its filtration weight. These maps intertwine the diagonal $\operatorname{GL}(V)$ action; the special fiber has the stated $\operatorname{GL}(V)^n$ action.

Write $\ell(m)=|\{i:m_i=1\}|$ and $d(m)=|\{i:m_i=2\}|$. In each polynomial degree $k$, the original quotient is proved isomorphic as a color representation to

$$
\bigoplus_{m\ \mathrm{admissible}}(C_m)_k\otimes
V^{\otimes\ell(m)}\otimes(\det V)^{d(m)}.
$$

Polynomiality, rationality and finite dimensionality are proved for the original and comparison actions before applying the representation-theory results. The resulting polynomially graded character is exactly (22):

$$
\operatorname{ch}_{\operatorname{GL}_2}(R_n^{(1,2)};q;u,v)
=\sum_{m\ \mathrm{admissible}}
\Bigl(\prod_i[c_i]_q\Bigr)(u+v)^{\ell(m)}(uv)^{d(m)}.
$$

The Schur decomposition proves the existence of an intertwining `Representation.Equiv`; the splitting is noncanonical. The multiplicity polynomial for $\operatorname{Sym}^a V\otimes(\det V)^b$ is

$$
\sum_{\substack{m\ \mathrm{admissible},\ 0\leq j\leq\ell(m)/2\\
\ell(m)-2j=a,\ d(m)+j=b}}
\left\{\binom{\ell(m)}j-\binom{\ell(m)}{j-1}\right\}
\prod_i[c_i]_q,
$$

with the second binomial coefficient defined to be zero at $j=0$. The summands are proved irreducible, and two summand labels give equivalent representations precisely when their two labels agree.

Finally, insertion of the largest letter proves the Eulerian recurrence for $E_n(a,b)=\sum_{\pi\in S_n}a^{\mathrm{asc}(\pi)}b^{\mathrm{des}(\pi)}$. Its comparison with the path transfer recurrence proves (23),

$$
\operatorname{ch}_{\operatorname{GL}_2}R_n^{(1,2)}(u,v)=E_n(1+u,1+v).
$$

Neither the filtration nor the module isomorphisms assert a simultaneous $S_n$-equivariant splitting.
