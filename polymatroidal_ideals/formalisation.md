# Characterizations of polymatroidal ideals

The object of this verification is *Characterizations of polymatroidal ideals*, in the version included here. Its main assertion is that an exchange rule for monomials can be recovered from either of two algebraic tests: linear resolutions after every monomial localization, or, for an equigenerated ideal, reverse lexicographic linear quotients for every variable order. The formalisation also treats the integral-closure theorem, the local exchange criterion, and the sharp homological and order-testing results in the later sections. This note explains what those statements mean in the code, how the important mathematical bridges are proved, and how to reproduce the checks.

## The mathematical objects retained in Lean

Let $V$ be a finite set of variables, let $k$ be any field, and put $S=k[x_i:i\in V]$. The coefficient field is an arbitrary parameter; no argument assumes characteristic zero, a finite field, or an ordered field. The ring is Mathlib's `MvPolynomial V k`, and an ideal is its ordinary `Ideal`. Exponents are finitely supported functions $V\to\mathbb N$. Since $V$ is finite, these are precisely the exponent vectors used in the paper.

The input predicate `IsMonomialIdeal` says that the ideal is spanned by coefficient-one monomials from an arbitrary set of exponents. It does not assume a finite presentation as part of the input. The module `reading/characterizations/monomial_normal_form.lean` proves that such an ideal has a finite minimal monomial generating family, proves uniqueness of that family, and identifies its members with the divisibility-minimal monomials in the ideal. Consequently the finite-family arguments apply to every monomial ideal in the statement of the paper.

For a family $B$, `IsPolymatroidal B` means exactly that its members have one degree and satisfy prescribed-donor exchange:

$$
a,b\in B,\quad a_i>b_i
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\exists j,\quad a_j<b_j,\quad a-e_i+e_j\in B.
$$

`IsPolymatroidalIdeal` means that an ideal is the actual ideal span of a polymatroidal family. This connects the exponent and polynomial representations. Neither a resolution nor an integral-closure assertion is part of the definition of polymatroidality.

For $U\subseteq V$, localization is `Ideal.map` along the polynomial homomorphism sending a retained variable to itself and every other variable to $1$. Its target is $k[x_i:i\in U]$, rather than a ring with unused variables silently left in place. The theorem `monomialLocalization_eq` identifies its minimal generators with the divisibility-minimal projections of $B$. Coordinate restriction, used later, is separately formalised: it keeps the generators supported on the retained coordinates and corresponds to setting the other variables to $0$.

### Resolutions and quotients

`HasLinearQuotients` is a condition on literal colon ideals. For each generator it requires the colon of the preceding ideal by that monomial to equal an ideal generated by variables. A proved equivalence converts this condition to the variable-witness rule used in the paper. Thus a convenient exponent test is used only after its algebraic meaning has been established.

A value of `LinearResolution I d` consists of finite free polynomial modules in each homological degree, polynomial-linear differentials, an augmentation with image $I$, and equalities of image and kernel expressing exactness. The differential matrices have homogeneous entries of degree one; the augmentation has degree $d$. In homological degree $i$, the corresponding graded free module therefore has shift $d+i$. The code constructs a genuine Mathlib projective resolution from these data. The property `HasLinearResolution I` is the existence of such a resolution.

The unit ideal and empty variable set are included. The zero ideal uses the paper's empty-generator convention and has the zero complex as a linear resolution. These cases are handled in the main ideal-level characterizations; they are not removed by a blanket nonzero assumption.

## Why local linearity forces exchange

The converse in Theorem 1.1(A) has a short mathematical core. For nonzero $I$, set

$$
r(U)=\min_{a\in B}\sum_{i\in U}a_i.
$$

Projection preserves these minima. If every localization is equigenerated, a generator of $I_{A\cup B}$ minimizing its degree on $A\cap B$ proves

$$
r(A)+r(B)\le r(A\cup B)+r(A\cap B).
$$

The local degrees therefore form a supermodular function.

The next step is where homology enters. A monomial $v\notin J$ with $x_iv\in J$ for every variable gives a top Koszul class of $S/J$. If $J$ has an $e$-linear resolution, comparison with that resolution forces $\deg v=e-1$. This is Lemma 2.2, proved using the actual Koszul complex and an actual resolution. It does not assume the desired degree constraint as a property of a surrogate socle.

Lemma 2.5 then recovers ideal membership from the subset degrees:

$$
I=\bigcap_{U\subseteq V}P_U^{r(U)},\qquad P_U=(x_i:i\in U).
$$

To understand the reverse containment, start with a missing monomial and choose a smallest localization in which it is still missing. In every coordinate it has some power multiple that belongs to the localized ideal. A maximal remaining multiple is a socle monomial, so its degree is one less than the local generator degree. The original monomial consequently violates the corresponding subset inequality.

Finally, the supermodular inequalities imply exchange. If every proposed move $a-e_i+e_j$ fails, each failure supplies a tight subset containing the donor $i$ and omitting the proposed receiver $j$. Tight sets are closed under intersection. Their intersection contains the strict surplus coordinate and no deficit coordinate, contradicting the same lower inequality for the target base.

These are Lemmas 2.4--2.6. The forward implication proves that exchange survives projection, constructs reverse lexicographic linear quotients, and builds the linear resolution by homogeneous mapping cones. The main ideal-level endpoint is

Characterizations.monomialIdeal_polymatroidal_iff_local_linear

Its hypotheses say only that the input is a monomial ideal over the specified finite-variable polynomial ring. The proof does not use Theorem 2.11 or Proposition 4.1. Corollary 2.10 then follows for every coefficient field: local linear resolutions over one field force exchange for the unique minimal exponent family, and that same family has local linear resolutions over any other field. The literal-ideal statement includes zero.

## The convex hull and the whole integral closure

For nonzero $I$, Theorem 2.11 identifies the generator hull under local equigeneration alone, even when some integer points are absent. Starting from the empty localization, the code lifts generators along each chain $\varnothing=U_0\subset U_1\subset\cdots\subset U_n=V$. Equigeneration determines the new coordinate at every step. This produces a generator satisfying $b(U_j)=r(U_j)$ for every prefix of the chosen variable order. Summation by parts shows that these generators minimize every real linear functional on the proposed polytope. Convex separation then proves the literal equality

$$
\operatorname{conv}(B)=\{x\in\mathbb R_{\ge0}^{V}:x(V)=d,\ x(U)\ge r(U)\text{ for every }U\}.
$$

The left side is Mathlib's real convex hull. The right side contains real points, not just rational or integral ones.

The ordinary ideal-integrality predicate is written explicitly as

$$
f^q+\sum_{i<q}c_i f^i=0,
\qquad q>0,\qquad c_i\in I^{q-i}.
$$

It applies to arbitrary polynomials $f$. Rational convex representations and denominator clearing prove the required monomial integrality statements. A separate least-subset-degree argument proves the reverse containment for every integral polynomial: a term below a required subset degree would make the leading power in its integral equation the unique term of least weight.

The result is the full ideal equality

$$
\overline I=\bigcap_{U\subseteq V}P_U^{r(U)},
$$

and the right side is proved equigenerated and polymatroidal. The modules `RealHull`, `RationalConvex`, `ClearDenominators`, `IntegralClosure`, and `PrimePowers` contain these bridges. The declaration `monomialIdeal_integralClosure` gives the literal-ideal formulation. The resulting characterization by integral closedness and local equigeneration includes zero in `monomialIdeal_polymatroidal_iff_integrallyClosed_all`.

For a small example, $(x^2,y^2)$ has equigenerated monomial localizations, and its generator hull is the segment from $(2,0)$ to $(0,2)$. The missing integer point $(1,1)$ accounts for the difference between the ideal and its integral closure $(x,y)^2$. The monomial $xy$ is a socle element of degree two, which is too high for a two-linear resolution. This is the distinction between local convex geometry and missing lattice points that the formal proof preserves.

## How the order characterization is proved

For a coordinate bound $C$, let $B_C=\{b\in B:b\le C\}$. The project proves Lemma 3.1 as an equivalence with a genuine finite linear presentation: the unit-exchange graph on each nonempty $B_C$ is connected exactly when the linear syzygies generate the presentation kernel. In a fixed multidegree the kernel is the coefficient-sum-zero subspace. Edge differences span that space precisely when the graph is connected.

Following a path in such a box turns any failed prescribed exchange into a failure at $\ell_1$-distance four. Final-pair tests first rule out failures having only one possible receiver. The surviving obstruction then involves four distinct coordinates and the four absent exchanges in Lemma 3.5. An order with the two receivers immediately before the donor makes the target earlier but leaves no possible variable colon witness. This proves the converse in Theorem 1.1(B), and also the exact local-exchange criterion of Theorem 3.3. The literal-ideal endpoint is `monomialIdeal_polymatroidal_iff_universal_quotients`.

The variable order is quantified over all permutations. For the extension in Corollary 3.10, the comparison is pure reverse lexicographic order, without a preliminary total-degree comparison. The proof derives equigeneration from the universal quotient condition. On an equigenerated family this order agrees with the graded reverse lexicographic term order in Theorem 1.1(B).

Theorem 3.12 handles every monomial term order. Its matching argument is one-sided: every matched exchange is feasible at the original base. The code proves the required matching through a finite clone matroid, rather than assuming symmetric base orderability. Additivity of the chosen monomial order then forces at least one feasible exchange to move to an earlier generator. The endpoints lie in `PureDegree`, `Presentation/Finite`, and `TermOrders/Characterization`.

## The homological tests use actual derived Tor

The shared `CommAlg` development constructs the exterior Koszul complex of the polynomial variables and proves that it is a projective resolution of the coefficient field. A polynomial monomial times an exterior basis vector has multidegree $a+\mathbf1_F$. The homogeneous pieces are subcomplexes, and their homology maps injectively into Mathlib's derived Tor. Their images form the actual multigrading and exhaust the whole module.

Some intermediate constructions compute $\operatorname{Tor}_i(k,I)$, whereas the paper writes $\operatorname{Tor}_i(I,k)$. The module `DerivedTorOrder` proves the change of convention using the degree-preserving tensor flip on the same resolution. It also proves preservation of the graded dimensions and completeness of the transported grading. The final theorems with suffix `paperTor` use the paper's argument order. Thus the vanishing conditions concern the full derived functor, not merely selected cycles or a numerical formula introduced by definition.

The key obstruction for Theorem 4.2 is a smallest nonpolymatroidal mixed coordinate minor $L$. Its missing generator has full support, so if it has degree $e$ in $m$ remaining variables then $m\le e$. A monomial socle class above that missing generator has degree $t\ge e$ and yields

$$
\operatorname{Tor}_{m-1}(L,k)_{t+m}\ne0.
$$

The index satisfies $m-1\le e-1$. An explicit graded Koszul comparison transports this class back to a localization of the original ideal. The formal obstruction proof uses this direct socle construction; it needs no regularity identity from Proposition 4.1.

Theorems 4.5 and 4.9 then control which localizations need to be examined. Enlarging a localization without changing its generator degree gives a polynomial extension. At a maximal such enlargement, restoring each omitted variable costs at least one degree. Supermodularity adds those costs. For a nonzero ideal generated in degree $d\ge2$, Theorem 4.9 tests precisely the sets with $|U|\ge2$ and $n-|U|\le d-1$. Its triangular bound is exactly

$$
i\le\min\{e_U-1,\ d-1-(n-|U|),\ |U|-1\}.
$$

Equigeneration is separately required at each tested localization. Unit localizations need no positive-degree check. If local equigeneration is already assumed, Theorem 4.5 reduces the localization band to $n-|U|\le d-2$. The exact minimum numbers of localizations are, respectively,

$$
\sum_{c=0}^{\min(d-1,n-2)}\binom nc
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
\sum_{c=0}^{\min(d-2,n-2)}\binom nc,
\qquad n,d\ge2.
$$

Without a degree bound, Corollary 4.8 gives the attained minimum $2^n-n-1$. The corresponding declarations retain these quantifiers and cutoffs:

polymatroidal_iff_degree_tests_paperTor
polymatroidal_iff_triangular_paperTor

Sharpness is also part of the project. For Proposition 4.3, let $J=(x^b:|b|=e,\ b\ne a)$, where $|a|=e$, $S=\operatorname{supp}(a)$, and $|S|\ge2$. For $|\alpha|>e+i$, the fine Betti number $\beta_{i,\alpha}(J)$ is one exactly when

$$
\alpha=a+\mathbf1_F,\qquad S\subseteq F\subseteq V,\qquad |F|=i+1
$$

for some $F$, and is zero otherwise. Summing the actual graded dimensions gives $\beta_{i,e+i+1}(J)=\binom{n-|S|}{i+1-|S|}$, with all other nonlinear total degrees zero. Lean states the out-of-range zero case separately, before using natural-number subtraction. The first nonlinear position is $|S|-1$. Every proper localization is the unit ideal. When $S=V$, the sole nonlinear entry is $\beta_{n-1,e+n}=1$; taking $e=n$ exhibits the sharp degree cutoff. The `Isolators` modules prove that the capped examples have exactly one localization without a linear resolution and calculate its first nonlinear position. At a set $U$ of codimension $c$ with $|U|\ge2$, the optimal degree of a locally equigenerated isolator is $c+2$. For proper $U$, an isolator with mixed generator degrees at that localization has optimal degree $c+1$. Arbitrary degree padding preserves the obstruction. These examples prove necessity of every individual test in the preceding counts, as well as the sharp initial-segment depths of Proposition 4.10.

## The exact number of orders and the rejection probability

The final section concerns recognition among equigenerated monomial ideals. The number $N(n)$ allows every generator degree, while $N(n,d)$ fixes that degree to $d$. The pair and triple isolators are proved to fail precisely the orders with their specified final pattern. They therefore establish necessity of the coverage conditions, not just examples of failure. The minimum test numbers are minima of actual finite families satisfying the universal testing condition; existence, attainment, and the lower bound against every other valid family are proved.

This gives $N(1)=0$, $N(2)=1$, $N(3)=6$, and $N(n)=3\binom n3$ for $n\ge4$. The fixed-degree theorem retains the bounded pattern size and the additional final-pair requirement when $d\ge n-1$. For $n\ge4$ and $d\ge n-2$ it also gives $N(n,d)=3\binom n3$. The counting and antichain lower bounds, the logarithmic upper bound, and the asymptotic conclusion are separate proved statements. More explicitly, when $2\le d\le n-2$, put $Q=n\binom{n-1}{2}\binom{n-3}{d-1}$. The bounds are

$$
3\binom{d+2}{3}\ \le\ N(n,d)\ \le\
\left\lceil\frac{d(d+1)(d+2)}{2}(\log Q+1)\right\rceil.
$$

For $M=N(n,d)$, $n\ge4$, and $d\ge2$, the signature bound is $n-1\le\binom M{\lfloor M/2\rfloor}$. The signatures of variables across the chosen orders form an antichain, giving the logarithmic lower bound. Together these yield $N(n,d)=\Theta_d(\log n)$ for every fixed $d\ge2$. The last uses Mathlib's actual `Asymptotics.IsTheta` at infinity. Its endpoint is `orderTestNumber_isTheta_log`.

The uniform probability is the proportion of failing permutations among all variable permutations. Theorem 5.6 proves its sharp lower bound

$$
\frac{2}{t(t+1)(t+2)},\qquad t=\min(d,n-2),
$$

for every nonpolymatroidal degree-$d$ ideal with $n\ge4$ and $d\ge2$. It also constructs ideals attaining equality, proves the sharp values $1/6$ in three variables and $1$ in two variables for every $d\ge2$. For $M$ independent orders, the actual uniform probability of missing a fixed nonpolymatroidal ideal is at most $(1-p)^M$, where $p$ is the applicable sharp lower bound. These results are in `ExactCoverage`, `DegreeCoverage`, `AsymptoticTests`, `Rejection`, and `SmallRejection`. The principal probability endpoints are `rejection_fraction_displayed` and `independent_tests_miss_bound`; `exists_sharp_rejection_displayed` supplies equality in the main range.

## The manuscript correction and the trusted boundary

The corrected manuscript makes two domain conventions explicit. The convex-hull description in Theorem 2.11 requires $I\ne0$, because its subset function is a minimum over the nonempty generator family. Its integral-closedness characterization also holds at zero, and the project states that case separately. Proposition 4.1 likewise requires a nonzero ideal for its finite regularity formula.

The latter point has an explicit formal counterexample to the unrestricted reading. At $I=0$, every $\mathfrak m^qI$ is zero and hence polymatroidal, so the least $q$ is $0$. The Tor-degree support is empty, so there is no attained finite regularity; with the usual extended convention, $\operatorname{reg}(0)=-\infty$. Consequently the unqualified formula $\min\{q:\mathfrak m^qI\text{ is polymatroidal}\}=\operatorname{reg}(I)-d$ fails there. The module `ZeroRegularity` proves this failure and the valid zero-ideal closure and finite-length statements. The complete nonzero Proposition 4.1 is proved as an additional result. The declaration `unrestricted_proposition41_extended_regularity_false` directly negates the unrestricted equality.

The main characterizations and sharp testing theorems keep their original parameter ranges. The corrected proof of Theorem 4.2 uses the socle argument described above. The integral-closure bar in Proposition 4.1 is retained: its Newton-polyhedron step asserts $\overline{\mathfrak m^qI}=\mathfrak m^q\overline I$, not an equality of the original ideals without taking closure.

The permitted logical axioms are `propext`, `Classical.choice`, and `Quot.sound`. The audit traverses checked declarations and rejects `sorryAx` and any additional mathematical axiom. A separate source review found no proof placeholders or external proof oracles. The transitive graphs expand the local `Characterizations`, `CommAlg`, and vendored `TauCeti` proofs down to the pinned Lean and Mathlib boundary. An independent statement review checks the mathematical meaning of the definitions and the full hypotheses of the endpoints; an axiom list alone could not establish that correspondence.
